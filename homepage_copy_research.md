# Care Plus homepage copy research

## Research question
How should the Care Plus homepage copy communicate value and move a shopper toward browsing second-hand phones without sounding generic or AI-generated?

## Findings

XtraCover’s storefront leads with a concrete operational promise: a quality-used-device marketplace backed by a 64-point quality check, warranty, and after-sales support. Its supporting proof points are specific and scannable: verified products, price guarantee, warranty duration, quality checks, free shipping, and cash on delivery. The useful pattern is to pair a clear category promise with measurable reassurance rather than broad adjectives.

SplitBase’s ecommerce landing-page guidance recommends a hero with a benefit-led headline, a subheadline that reinforces the value proposition, and a clear CTA. It argues that the next sections should explain the problem solved and the benefits, then build trust with short, informative content. It also stresses relevance to the audience and researching the questions and objections customers actually have. The page should be as long as needed to resolve those concerns, not padded with generic copy.

## Copy direction for Care Plus

The current copy uses broad phrases such as “Exceptional phones. Unbeatable prices.” and “Find your next favourite device, without the first-hand price.” These are polished but interchangeable. Care Plus should instead lead with a specific buyer outcome: getting a known phone model for less, without guessing about condition. The subheadline should state the proof that Care Plus controls directly: each phone is tested, condition-labelled, and covered by the site’s stated warranty and return terms. The primary CTA should be an action phrase tied to the catalogue, such as “Shop phones,” with a supporting CTA for selling.

Recommended message hierarchy:

1. Outcome-led headline: “The phone you want, for less.”
2. Specific reassurance: “Tested second-hand iPhones, Samsung and more, with clear condition grades, warranty coverage, and delivery across India.”
3. Primary CTA: “Shop phones.”
4. Secondary CTA: “Sell your phone.”
5. Category bridge: “Start with what you know.”
6. Category rail labels should describe shopper intent: “Best value,” “Pro cameras,” “Flagship Android,” and “Everyday phones.”
7. Featured section should say why the list is shown: “Good phones, picked for today.”
8. Bottom CTA should remove filler and say: “See every phone in the catalogue.”
